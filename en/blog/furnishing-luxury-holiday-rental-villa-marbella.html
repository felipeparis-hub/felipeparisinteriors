Furnishing a Holiday Rental Villa in Marbella

How to furnish a luxury holiday rental villa in Marbella: what to prioritise, durable materials, guest comfort and the mistakes that cost you bookings.

By Felipe Paris, interior designer in Marbella

When an owner tells me they plan to rent out their villa, the whole conversation changes. We are no longer furnishing a home: we are furnishing a product that a different group of guests judges every week, compares with other villas on the same platform and reviews in public. Competition in high-end holiday rentals on the Costa del Sol is intense, and the difference between a villa that books itself and one that keeps cutting its rate is very often the interior.

I spent years in Costa del Sol real estate and then trained for five years with Roche Bobois in France, so I have seen both sides: what a demanding guest looks for, and what actually survives real use. This guide sums up how I set priorities when the goal is to make a luxury villa in Marbella profitable without losing its character.

1. Define the guest before choosing a single piece

A villa in La Zagaleta for families staying three weeks in August is not furnished like a Puerto Banús penthouse for groups of friends on a long weekend. Before we talk about style, I go through these questions with the owner:

  • How many people sleep in the house, and how many sit down to eat at the same time?
  • Families with children, couples, groups of friends or business travellers?
  • Is the villa rented all year or only in high season, and does the owner use it too?
  • Who handles cleaning and maintenance between stays?

The answers set the number of seats in the living room, the size of the dining table, the fabrics, and even how much lockable storage the owner needs for personal belongings.

2. Spend where the photos are

Guests book with their eyes. The first images in a listing are usually the living room, the pool terrace, the master bedroom and the kitchen. That is where I put most of the budget:

  1. Living room: a quality sofa, properly sized, arranged to face the view. It is the most photographed and most used piece in the house.
  2. Terrace and pool area: in Marbella people live outdoors for much of the year. A generous outdoor dining set and a comfortable lounge area matter as much as the indoor living room.
  3. Master bedroom: a great bed, hotel-grade linen and warm lighting. It is what reviews mention most.
  4. Dining room: one table for the whole group, with no mismatched chairs added at the last minute.

Secondary bedrooms, corridors and bathrooms must be spotless, but they can be simpler. A plain, well-resolved guest room beats five rooms that are only half finished.

3. Choose materials for heavy use

The most common mistake I see in rental villas is choosing materials meant for a house used two weeks a year. With guests changing every week, sun cream, children and people walking in from the pool, wear multiplies. I covered this in my guide to seafront villa interior design in Marbella; for rentals, my rules are:

  • Removable covers on sofas and chairs wherever possible, so they can be cleaned or replaced without changing the piece.
  • Performance or high-resistance fabrics in high-traffic areas, and true outdoor fabrics on the terrace.
  • Easy-care surfaces for dining and coffee tables: lacquer, ceramic or treated stone rather than delicate woods or unsealed marble.
  • Mid-tones rather than pure white on the pieces people touch most. Sand beige or stone grey age far better.

The Roche Bobois furniture I work with comes in a wide range of fabrics for each model, so you can keep a designer piece and simply choose the right cover for the use.

4. Invest in comfort, not fragile objects

Put the budget into what guests actually feel: good mattresses and pillows, deep sofas where the whole family fits for a film night, blackout curtains in bedrooms (with Costa del Sol light, their absence is a frequent complaint), reading lamps by every bed and dimmable lighting in the living room. Keep decoration to a few large, sturdy pieces: a strong rug, a couple of sculptural lamps, art properly fixed to the wall. Fewer loose objects means fewer breakages, faster cleaning and cleaner photos.

5. Plan the turnover logistics

A rental villa is also furnished for the cleaning team:

  • Duplicate textile sets: cushion covers, bed linen and, where the model allows, a second cover for the main sofa.
  • Furniture that is easy to move, or raised on legs so a vacuum fits underneath.
  • A lockable owner's cupboard for personal items and spare linen.
  • A photo inventory of every room, so the house is always reset the same way.

Andalusian rules for tourist rentals also set equipment requirements, so check with your property manager what your licence needs before buying anything.

6. Keep a recognisable identity

Among hundreds of villas with pools and sea views, personality is what makes guests remember yours. A coherent thread, a palette that works with the light, and two or three design pieces that become the image of the listing are enough.

A great rental villa is recognised in the first photo and remembered for how well people sleep in it.

7. Mistakes to avoid

  • Furnishing with leftovers from other homes.
  • Picking the sofa from a catalogue photo without checking size and depth.
  • Leaving the terrace until the budget has run out.
  • Buying everything in pure white because it looks good on day one.
  • Forgetting storage for the owner.
