Furnishing your Marbella home from abroad: a guide for foreign owners

How to furnish a villa in Marbella without living here: what I need to start, renders on your photos, phases, timing, deliveries and mistakes to avoid.

By Felipe Paris, interior designer specialising in Roche Bobois in Marbella

Go to my website

Many of Marbella's villas belong to owners who do not live here all year round. They buy in spring, sign at the notary, fly back to Amsterdam, Munich, London, Stockholm or New York… and then face the same question: how do you furnish an entire house from two thousand kilometres away?

I know this situation well. I spent years in Costa del Sol real estate working with international buyers, and today I design interiors for many of them. Here is how I run a complete project without you having to be here for every step.

1. What I need to get started

You do not need to fly in to begin. With the following I can start working from day one:

  • The floor plan, even if it is the one from the developer's brochure or the purchase deed.
  • Photos and a video of every room, taken from the doorway while turning slowly. If you are not in Marbella, I can take them myself during a visit.
  • Key measurements: windows, doors, ceiling heights and the position of sockets and light points.
  • How you will use the house: how many people, how many weeks a year, and whether you will rent it out when you are away.

With that I prepare the layout of each room and a first selection of pieces.

2. See the finished home before buying anything

The natural fear of any remote owner is getting it wrong without having seen anything in person. That is why I work with renders on top of your own photos: you see your living room, with your light and your views, furnished with the proposed pieces. You can see a real example in the villa project in Elviria, which started from a house still under works.

Then we review it together on a video call. I show you fabrics, finishes and colours, we compare options and adjust whatever is needed. Changing a sofa in an image takes minutes; changing it once it has been delivered takes a lot longer.

3. Decide in phases

Not everything has to be ready on day one. I usually suggest two phases:

  1. The essentials for your first stay: beds, sofa, dining table, a basic terrace and lighting.
  2. Everything else once you have lived in the house: rugs, statement pieces, decoration and secondary rooms.

If the villa will be rented out, plan for heavy use from the start; I explain how in my guide to furnishing a holiday rental villa in Marbella.

4. Timing and logistics

What makes a remote project difficult is not the design but the coordination. My rules:

  • Set a target date (the first week you will spend in the house) and plan backwards. Made-to-order pieces take weeks to produce, so decisions need to be made with time to spare.
  • Decide who opens the door: a property manager, the community concierge or someone you trust with a key. Deliveries are coordinated with that person.
  • Assembly and placement according to the approved plan, so you do not arrive to a house full of boxes.
  • A final check with photos and video of every room before you arrive.

5. One point of contact, in your language

I speak English, Spanish, French and Italian. You have a single person who knows the house, the plan and the budget, and who answers you on WhatsApp. No middlemen, and no messages lost between suppliers.

6. Common mistakes when furnishing from abroad

  • Buying from a catalogue without measuring: the sofa that looked perfect does not fit the wall, or looks lost in the room.
  • Underestimating volume: in high-ceilinged rooms everything looks smaller; I cover this in my guide to the sofa for a double-height living room.
  • Forgetting the sun: fabrics and woods that fade in a single season. More on this in seafront villa materials.
  • Leaving the terrace until last, when in Marbella you live outdoors for much of the year.
  • Not planning who receives deliveries, and ending up with furniture waiting in a warehouse.

7. Every area has its own character

An apartment on the Golden Mile is not furnished like a villa in Sierra Blanca or a family home in Nueva Andalucía. If you are still choosing where to buy, my guide to furnishing a villa in Marbella area by area may help.
