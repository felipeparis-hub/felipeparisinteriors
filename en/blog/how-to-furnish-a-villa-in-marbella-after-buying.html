How to Furnish a Villa in Marbella After Buying It

A step-by-step guide to turning an empty Costa del Sol villa into a home: lifestyle, measurements, concept, 3D, lead times and outdoors.

By Felipe Paris, interior designer in Marbella

Buying a villa in Marbella is the end of a long process: viewings, negotiation, the notary. But the day you get the keys another project begins, often just as important: turning an empty house into a home that is a pleasure to live in and that lives up to the property. This guide sums up how I approach it with the owners I work with.

1. Start with how you will live in the house

Before looking at a single sofa, answer a few simple questions:

  • Will it be your main home, a second home or a holiday rental?
  • How many people will use it at once, and how often do you have guests?
  • Do you spend more time in the living room, on the terrace or in the kitchen?

A holiday villa needs hard-wearing, easy-care fabrics; a main residence can afford more delicate pieces. This one decision shapes the whole project.

2. Measure and photograph every room

Volumes are deceptive in Costa del Sol villas: double-height living rooms, huge windows and terraces that are part of daily life. A sofa that looks enormous in the showroom can feel small in an 80 m² living room. That is why the first step of any serious project is to measure every room, corridor and access, and note sockets, windows and light points.

3. Define a concept, not a shopping list

The most common mistake is buying piece by piece with no common thread. The result is a house full of beautiful furniture that does not talk to each other. What works is deciding first:

  • A colour palette that works with Marbella's light and with the existing floor.
  • Two or three leading materials, for example travertine, light wood and natural fabrics.
  • One anchor piece per room: the living-room sofa, the dining table, the master bed.

4. See it in 3D before you buy

You no longer need to imagine the result. With a furnished floor plan and 3D renders of every room you see the finished house before ordering anything: proportions, colours, how the evening light falls on the living room. It is the best way to avoid expensive mistakes. You can see an example in the before and after of a 600 m² villa.

5. Allow for lead times

Quality furniture, and especially pieces made to measure in the fabrics and finishes you choose, is not delivered overnight. Always ask about production and delivery times before confirming an order, and plan the project backwards from the date you want to move in or start renting.

6. Think about the outdoors from day one

In Marbella the terrace is another room. Outdoor furniture, shade and garden lighting should be part of the same project, with the same palette, so that inside and outside read as one house.

7. One single point of contact

Coordinating measurements, orders, deliveries and assembly across several suppliers, often remotely if you do not live here all year, is what wears people out most. Having one person who runs the project from start to finish, in your language, saves time and mistakes.

A well-furnished home is not the one with the most furniture, but the one with the right pieces in the right place.
