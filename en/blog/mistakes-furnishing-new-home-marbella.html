9 Mistakes When Furnishing a Newly Bought Home in Marbella

The most expensive mistakes when furnishing a newly bought home in Marbella and how to avoid them: measurements, lead times, scale, terrace, light and one point of contact.

By Felipe Paris, interior designer specialising in Roche Bobois in Marbella

Go to my website

You have just signed at the notary, you have the keys and an empty house in Marbella. It is the most exciting moment… and also the moment when the most expensive decisions are made. After years in Costa del Sol real estate, and now as an interior designer specialising in Roche Bobois, I have seen the same mistakes again and again. Almost all of them can be avoided with a bit of method.

If you want the full step-by-step process, I explain it in how to furnish a villa in Marbella after buying it. Here I focus on the opposite: what not to do.

1. Buying the sofa before having a floor plan

This is mistake number one. The sofa arrives, it is beautiful, and then you discover it blocks half the window or the way out to the terrace. Before choosing a single piece you need a plan with the real measurements and a thought-through layout: where the family sits, what they look at, where people walk.

2. Furnishing room by room with no common concept

The living room first, the bedroom months later, the terrace the following year. The result is often a house with three styles that do not talk to each other. A shared colour palette and a few common materials (one wood, one stone, one fabric tone) make the house read as a whole, even if you furnish it in stages.

3. Measuring the room and forgetting the way in

Doors, corridors, stairs, the building's lift, the turn at the entrance. A 260 cm dining table or a one-piece sofa may not get in, even if the room is huge. I always check this before an order is placed.

4. Underestimating lead times

Quality furniture, especially pieces made to order in your fabric and finish, is produced for you and takes weeks. If you want the house ready for the summer or for the family's arrival, the order has to go out with time to spare. Ordering in May for July usually ends with a half-furnished house.

5. Getting the scale wrong

Living rooms in Marbella villas tend to be large, sometimes double height. Furniture designed for a city flat gets lost in them: the sofa looks small and the room looks empty. I write about this in my article on the sofa for a double-height living room.

6. Leaving the terrace until last

On the Costa del Sol the terrace is used for much of the year and is, in practice, another living room. If it waits until the budget has been spent, you end up with four odd chairs in the most beautiful space in the house.

7. Thinking about light once everything is bought

Natural light here is very strong and changes a lot through the day, and in the evening the house lives under lamps. Colours and fabrics should be chosen in that light, not in a shop's. I explain this in colour palettes for a home in Marbella.

8. Deciding without seeing it all together

Choosing each piece separately, from different catalogues, without ever seeing the whole. It is the cause of most returns and regrets. That is why I work with scale plans and 3D renders of every room: clients see their home furnished before buying anything and change what they do not like on screen, not when the truck has already arrived.

9. Managing everything from abroad with no single contact

Many owners live outside Spain and coordinate several shops, carriers and fitters from home. It is exhausting, and it is where measurement and date mistakes happen. Having one person who knows the house, the plan and the budget makes everything simpler. More in my guide to furnishing your Marbella home from abroad.

How I avoid them in my projects

My way of working is designed precisely to avoid these mistakes: a visit or video call, a scale plan with real measurements, a complete proposal with Roche Bobois furniture and renders of every room, then the order, delivery and placement coordinated by me. A recent example is a client in Cádiz who furnished almost her whole home after seeing it in 3D.

A well-furnished home does not start in the shop: it starts on the floor plan.
