Furnishing a Villa in Marbella, Area by Area

How to furnish a villa in Marbella by area: Golden Mile, Puerto Banús, Nueva Andalucía, Sierra Blanca, Elviria, Los Monteros, Río Real and Guadalmina.

By Felipe Paris, interior designer in Marbella

When it comes to homes, Marbella is not one place. A beachfront villa on the Golden Mile, a penthouse in Puerto Banús and a house between golf courses in Nueva Andalucía have different light, views, sizes and ways of living. So before choosing a single piece, the first thing I look at is where the house is.

I have spent years in these neighbourhoods, first in real estate and now furnishing villas with Roche Bobois furniture. This guide sums up how I approach furniture in each area of Marbella. I don't do building work: I work with the house as it is and make sure every piece suits its architecture and its setting.

The Golden Mile

Between Marbella town and Puerto Banús, the Golden Mile brings together beachfront villas, classic homes from the seventies and eighties and very contemporary residences. What they share is the light: plenty of it, strong, and usually from the sea.

  • Living rooms open to the terrace: low, deep sofas facing the view, never with their back to the windows.
  • Fabrics that cope with the sun: sand, stone and off-white tones, with covers that are easy to clean.
  • Fewer pieces, more presence: a sculptural sofa such as Sacha Lakic's Bubble, or a Mah Jong, the frameless sofa Hans Hopf designed in 1971, can hold a large living room without filling it.

Puerto Banús

Apartments and penthouses with terraces dominate here, often used as holiday or second homes. Space is used differently:

  • Modular pieces that fit narrower living rooms and can be rearranged.
  • The terrace as a second living room: outdoor dining and a lounge area facing the port or the sea.
  • Easy-care materials, especially if the property is rented. I go into detail in my guide to furnishing a holiday rental villa.

Nueva Andalucía

The so-called Golf Valley, with Las Brisas, Los Naranjos and Aloha, mixes traditional Andalusian villas with large contemporary homes. It is an area of families and international buyers who live here much of the year.

  • Double-height living rooms: the sofa has to be chosen for the volume, not just the floor area. I wrote about it in choosing a sofa for a double-height living room.
  • Dining rooms for many: a table like Fabrice Berrux's Bolero works well for long lunches with family and friends.
  • Indoor and outdoor in harmony: golf views call for greens, wood and natural fabrics.

Sierra Blanca

On the hillside above the Golden Mile, Sierra Blanca is one of Marbella's most exclusive gated communities. Large villas, many of them contemporary, with open views of the sea and, on clear days, the African coast.

  • Scale: generous rooms need large pieces and generous compositions, not small furniture spread around.
  • Zones within the living room: seating, reading and dining, each with its own rug and light.
  • Designer pieces: a coffee table with character, such as Christophe Delcourt's Palatine in walnut and travertine, anchors the whole space.

Elviria and Los Monteros

East of Marbella, among pine woods and beaches, Elviria and Los Monteros are quiet residential areas with family villas and beachfront homes. Life here is everyday rather than seasonal.

  • Comfort first: generous sofas, reading armchairs and dining rooms for daily use.
  • One thread through the house: repeating woods and materials from room to room so the villa reads as a whole.

You can see a real example in the before and after of a villa in Elviria, furnished from top to bottom with Roche Bobois.

Río Real

Around the Río Real golf course, east of the centre, there are spacious villas and apartments surrounded by greenery. A warm style works best: textured fabrics, natural woods and a palette that talks to the green of the course.

Guadalmina and San Pedro Alcántara

To the west, Guadalmina combines established villas by the golf course and the sea with newer homes, many lived in all year round. I prioritise durable, comfortable, timeless pieces, made for many years of use rather than one season.

Beyond Marbella

I also work in Benahavís and La Zagaleta, Estepona, Sotogrande, Mijas and Málaga, and remotely with owners who live abroad.
