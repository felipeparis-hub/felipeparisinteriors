Before and after: a villa in Elviria, Marbella, from site to render

A real project in Elviria, Marbella: photos of the building site and the rendered proposal with Roche Bobois furniture and full styling, room by room.

By Felipe Paris, interior designer in Marbella

Some projects are easier to explain with pictures than with words. This is one of them: a villa under renovation in Elviria, on the east side of Marbella, which I first visited while there was still scaffolding in the living room, bags of plaster on the floor and cables hanging from the walls. The owner asked for something very specific: to see her home furnished before making any decision.

Here is the result, room by room. On the left, the real photo of the building site, exactly as I took it on my phone. On the right, the rendered proposal on top of that same photo, with the furniture we chose and the full styling.

The starting point: a house under works

When someone buys or renovates a villa in Marbella, they almost always face the same situation: they have the plans, they know the size of every room, but they cannot picture how they will actually live there. That is completely normal. An empty double-height living room with a dusty floor looks nothing like a finished one.

That is why my method always starts the same way: photos of every room, the floor plan and a long conversation about how the family lives. With that I prepare a layout proposal, choose the pieces and build the images on top of the real photos. The client does not see a generic house from a catalogue: they see their house, with its own light, windows and views.

The double-height living room

The living room is the heart of the house: a pitched ceiling, a large house-shaped window facing the garden and glass doors on both sides. In a space like this the classic mistake is a sofa that is too small, pushed against a wall. We did the opposite: a Conversation modular sofa in an L shape, upholstered in beige Ricochet fabric, anchoring the seating area in the corner by the window; two Mica armchairs in ivory bouclé opposite; and the Palatine coffee table in walnut with a travertine inlay.

Underneath, a round Abysse rug in brown wool. The round shape matters: it softens the straight lines of the architecture and creates a warm island on a very pale stone floor.

The styling does the rest: large artworks in sand and ochre tones, a brass floor lamp by the armchairs, linen cushions, a throw, books, an olive tree in a terracotta pot and linen curtains filtering the afternoon light. That is what turns a beautiful living room into one you want to live in.

The dining room

For the dining room we proposed the 260 cm rectangular Bolero table, with a Calacatta marble top and oak base, surrounded by Circa chairs, plus the Palatine sideboard in walnut with a travertine detail, echoing the coffee table in the living room. Repeating materials from room to room is what gives a home its unity.

Above the table, a large paper pendant brings the scale of the ceiling down and makes dinners feel more intimate. A laid table, flowers, candles: it really helps the client to see the room "in use".

The study under the roof

Upstairs there is a room with exposed timber beams that the family wanted as an office and meeting room. We chose a 180 cm round Bolero marble table, Nixon chairs in cognac Samoa leather, a Margaux sofa in beige for guests and the Elanta TV unit. A round jute rug under the table and a large plant in the corner bring warmth to a space that felt cold during the works.

The entrance hall and the landing

The hall is the first thing you see when you walk in. Here a few well-chosen things were enough: an upholstered bench under the window, a round mirror, a long jute runner, a large vase with dried branches and a wall light.

On the upstairs landing, with its round windows, we proposed the Celta bookcase in caramel walnut, a reading armchair and a table lamp. The afternoon sun coming through the porthole windows draws circles of light on the wall: an architectural detail that deserved to take centre stage.

Why I work this way

Seeing the finished home before buying completely changes the way people decide. The client can compare options (for this project I prepared two different dining tables and two TV units), adjust colours and understand the real proportions of each piece in their own space. Doubts are solved on screen, not when the sofa has already been delivered.

A note on honesty: the "after" images are a rendered proposal generated with AI on top of the real photos of the site. They are meant to show the atmosphere; the final shapes and finishes of each piece are those of its official product sheet. The accompanying decoration (art, plants, textiles) is indicative.
